## Executive summary

ArtLife's situation is unusually well defined, and it shapes the whole shortlist. ArtLife ([artlife.com](https://artlife.com/about/)) is a Miami-headquartered, online-first blue-chip gallery founded in 2015 by Avery Andon, trading Warhol, Haring, Basquiat and Picasso alongside Banksy, KAWS, Alec Monopoly, Space Invader, Os Gemeos and RETNA. It already runs a sophisticated Banksy demand engine — a dedicated [Banksy artist page](https://artlife.com/artists/banksy/), a [consignment funnel](https://artlife.com/sell/), and a deep editorial library including a [Pest Control authentication guide](https://artlife.com/news/pest-control-authentication/) and a Banksy investment guide.

That produces a precise diagnosis. **ArtLife's Banksy credibility is institutional and editorial; it is not personified.** Every serious competitor in this market sells through a named human being — MyArtBroker has Joe Syer and Jasper Tordoff, Andipa has Acoris Andipa, Hang-Up has Ben Cotton, GraffitiStreet has Rosh Boroumand. ArtLife has a brand and a content library but no publicly named Banksy specialist. Secondly, **ArtLife is demand-rich and supply-constrained by geography**: Banksy consignment supply is overwhelmingly concentrated in the UK and Europe, while ArtLife sits in Miami. The junior partner should therefore supply three things: a *name* the market already trusts, *UK/European supply-side access*, and the temperament to build a book inside a digital-first business rather than a traditional gallery.

Applying that lens, the highest-scoring individuals are not automatically the right approaches. The four strongest Banksy operators found — Joe Syer (88), Acoris Andipa (83), Ben Cotton (80) and Gareth Williams (80) — are each either a principal of a directly competing business or too established to be a "junior" partner. They are mapped here as competitive intelligence, referees and potential relationships, not as realistic hires. The genuinely actionable candidates sit slightly below them: **Jasper Tordoff**, a named Banksy specialist who is an employee rather than an owner; **Rosh Boroumand**, a founder whose online-to-gallery trajectory mirrors ArtLife's own; **Danny Herbert**, who carries the only hard sales numbers in the entire dataset; **Taylor Curry**, the strongest US-based candidate; and **Huw Lougher**, whose KPMG-to-founder path and online-first model is the closest structural analogue to ArtLife anywhere in the market.

One exclusion deserves emphasis. **Robin Barton of Bankrobber has among the deepest Banksy transaction records of anyone identified — and should not be approached.** His business is built on selling Banksy works removed from walls, which Pest Control has stated it will not authenticate and from which [Banksy has publicly disassociated himself](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/street-artist-banksy-calls-exhibition-london-disgusting-n89196). That is structurally incompatible with a gallery whose entire positioning rests on authentication.

## Research methodology

**Approach.** I mapped the Banksy commercial ecosystem first — auction houses with urban/street-art departments, Banksy-active galleries and brokerages, exhibition organisers — and then worked backwards from institutions to the named individuals running their commercial functions. This "search by employer, then by person" method surfaces operators who do the work but are not individually famous, which is exactly the junior-partner profile.

**Tools.** Apify was checked for first, as instructed. **No `APIFY_TOKEN` or equivalent credential exists in this environment, no Apify CLI config is present, and the egress proxy rejected `api.apify.com` outright under organisation policy.** Apify spend was therefore **$0**, far below the $25 ceiling. Research proceeded through search engines only, since direct page fetching was also blocked (see the limitation notice above). Where I needed to read a firm's team page, I used domain-scoped searches as a proxy for fetching — querying the search index restricted to a single domain, which surfaces individual staff-page URLs and titles.

**Evidence discipline.** Raw capture is held separately from evaluation: `banksy_candidate_evidence.csv` records one row per material claim with source, quality rating and confidence, so every conclusion in this document is traceable. I have consistently separated **employer accomplishments from individual accomplishments** — Phillips' £4.4m Editions result and Maddox's £7.6m Banksy figure appear in the evidence file explicitly flagged as departmental or firm-level and not attributable to any individual.

**What I refused to do.** No age is inferred anywhere; "rising" is assessed purely from career-stage evidence such as founding dates, promotion trails and tenure. No protected characteristic informs any ranking. No private contact details are recorded — contact routes are firm contact pages and public professional profiles only. No one was contacted. Critically, **I have kept "handled Banksy works" and "connected to Pest Control" rigorously separate**: several candidates demonstrate competence at *navigating* Pest Control's certification process, and in no case is that described as a relationship with Pest Control Office. Where a promotional article asserted such a connection (Maddox's creative director), I recorded it as an unverified promotional claim and excluded it from scoring.

## Market and ecosystem observations

**1. The Banksy trade is personified to an unusual degree.** In most secondary markets the firm is the brand. In Banksy, the specialist is. MyArtBroker, Andipa, Hang-Up and GraffitiStreet all lead with a named individual who appears in broadcast media. This is a direct strategic argument for ArtLife hiring a *public* specialist rather than a back-office one, and it means broadcast and publishing fluency is a genuine commercial asset, not a vanity metric.

**2. Supply, not demand, is the binding constraint — and supply is British.** The dense cluster of Banksy dealers sits in London and southern England (Knightsbridge, Hoxton, Mayfair, Brighton, Bristol, Chichester). Original Banksy consignments surface through UK collector networks formed in the 2000s at Pictures on Walls, Santa's Ghetto and the early gallery shows. A Miami gallery cannot originate that supply from Miami.

**3. Authentication is the market's single axis of risk, and it splits the field cleanly.** Pest Control is the only authenticating body, and it will not authenticate works removed from walls. This produces two distinct populations of Banksy dealer: those who build on certified provenance, and those who trade removed street works. The second group carries Banksy's own public repudiation. For a gallery positioned as ArtLife is, that line is not negotiable.

**4. The auction houses are the best talent pool, and they are under-mined.** Urban art departments — Bonhams, Tate Ward, Heritage, Artcurial, Phillips Editions, Roseberys, Julien's — concentrate exactly the skills ArtLife needs: consignment origination, condition and provenance assessment, valuation discipline, and repeat consignor relationships. Auction professionals also move more readily than gallery owners, because they do not own equity in what they would leave.

**5. "Art investment" marketing is a compliance tripwire.** Several fast-growing galleries in this space market artworks on investment-return grounds. One — Maddox — is now defending a US civil complaint alleging knowingly inflated valuations. Any candidate from that cohort needs specific diligence on their personal role in valuation practice, regardless of the merits of the case.

**6. The UK market is consolidating and dislocating simultaneously.** Enter Gallery (Brighton's oldest independent, and by its own account the first to sell Banksy prints) is reported to be closing; Steve Lazarides exited gallery life in 2019 and later sold his Banksy archive at Julien's. Dislocation frees people. Timing an approach to coincide with it is the cheapest recruiting advantage available.

## 1. Jasper Tordoff — Specialist Broker (Banksy Specialist), MyArtBroker, London
**Score 78/100 · Evidence: Medium · Banksy connection: Direct · Priority: Immediate**

Jasper Tordoff is the most precisely role-shaped candidate in this research, and the reason is structural rather than biographical: he is a *named Banksy specialist who does not own the business he works for*. Every other individual with an equivalent public Banksy profile — Syer, Andipa, Cotton, Boroumand — is a founder or principal, which makes them nearly impossible to recruit. Tordoff carries the credential without the equity handcuff. His [MyArtBroker profile](https://www.myartbroker.com/team/jasper-tordoff) describes deep expertise in the blue-chip print market "from early Lowry works to Banksy" with particular strength in print market dynamics, covering the acquisition and sale of secondary-market Banksy alongside Hockney, Warhol and Riley. He came in via Christie's, where the firm states he began in the Modern British department — an auction-house grounding in cataloguing and valuation discipline that matters for a role touching provenance and condition.

What distinguishes him commercially is media standing. He appears on national broadcast as a go-to Banksy voice, including [Sky News coverage of Banksy's London Zoo series](https://www.myartbroker.com/videos/articles/sky-news-banksy-london-zoo-video). For ArtLife specifically this is the missing asset: ArtLife has built an impressive Banksy content library but has no human face attached to it, while MyArtBroker's model demonstrates exactly how a named specialist converts media presence into inbound consignments. Tordoff already operates inside a technology-led brokerage that explicitly positions itself against traditional gallery practice — meaning ArtLife's online-first model would read to him as familiar rather than as a downgrade from a Mayfair shopfront.

**Score breakdown** — Banksy experience 23/25 · Sales & deal-closing 14/20 · Network 12/15 · Entrepreneurial 9/15 · Provenance & risk 8/10 · International 3/5 · Availability 4/5 · ArtLife fit 5/5

**Strongest supporting evidence.** Named Banksy specialist at the firm that calls itself the largest private dealer of Banksy's work ([team page](https://www.myartbroker.com/team/jasper-tordoff), [our people](https://www.myartbroker.com/our-people)); independent broadcast appearances as a Banksy expert; prior Christie's employment; corroborating [public LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jasper-tordoff-07b600137/) listing Specialist Broker.

**Gaps requiring verification.** *This is the central one:* **no evidence of partner-level P&L ownership, and no named transactions or sales figures attributable to him.** His entrepreneurial score is the lowest of the immediate-priority group precisely because nothing in the public record shows he has built anything. Also unverified: the size and portability of his personal client book; his Christie's dates and exact role; whether "Banksy specialist" reflects a formal designation or marketing positioning. **Why he may fit ArtLife.** He supplies the single thing ArtLife's Banksy practice most conspicuously lacks — a credible named human — and he supplies it from inside the competitor whose playbook ArtLife's editorial strategy already resembles. He is at the career stage where a partnership is a genuine step up rather than a lateral move.

**Tailored outreach angle.** Lead with the asymmetry: at MyArtBroker he is one specialist among several inside someone else's brand; at ArtLife he would *be* the Banksy practice, with a US collector base and a Miami platform already generating demand he currently has no access to. Frame it as ownership of a market, not a job.

**Potential concerns and conflicts.** Likely bound by confidentiality and possibly non-solicitation terms covering MyArtBroker's client book — take employment-law advice before any conversation about clients. It is not, and must never be represented as, a relationship with Pest Control Office.** For a gallery whose entire Banksy proposition rests on verified authenticity, that operational fluency is the competence that prevents expensive mistakes.

**Score breakdown** — Banksy experience 23/25 · Sales & deal-closing 14/20 · Network 11/15 · Entrepreneurial 13/15 · Provenance & risk 9/10 · International 3/5 · Availability 2/5 · ArtLife fit 4/5

His entry's value to ArtLife is as a benchmark for what two decades of Banksy relationship-building yields, and as a potential institutional counterparty — Andipa's museum-exhibition model is a supply-side tactic ArtLife could replicate independently.

**Score breakdown** — Banksy experience 25/25 · Sales & deal-closing 18/20 · Network 13/15 · Entrepreneurial 10/15 · Provenance & risk 9/10 · International 4/5 · Availability 1/5 · ArtLife fit 3/5

**Strongest supporting evidence.** Gallery publicly positioned as a Banksy secondary-market specialist since 2005 ([Andipa](https://andipagallery.com/), [staff directory](http://andipa.com/info/staff)); independent interview evidence of two decades of Banksy dealing; documented museum exhibition curation ([Rome](https://andipa.com/node/1798)); a published [Pest Control explainer](https://andipaeditions.com/pest-control/) evidencing authentication fluency.

**Tailored outreach angle.** Institutional rather than recruiting: a dealer-to-dealer relationship, or a conversation about how Andipa converts museum curation into consignment flow — a model ArtLife could adopt through its existing MOCA Bangkok and ICA Miami relationships.
