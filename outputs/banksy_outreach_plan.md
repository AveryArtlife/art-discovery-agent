## Before you use this

**Verify first, approach second.** Because no source page could be opened during this research (see `banksy_research_notes.md` §1), every claim underpinning these openers should be confirmed before you repeat it to the person. Citing a fact back to a candidate that turns out to be wrong is the fastest way to lose credibility with a specialist.

**Three candidates require a decision before contact:** Tim Luke (current employer genuinely unknown), Arnaud Oliveux (role evidence dates from 2018), and anyone at Maddox Gallery (live litigation — not in this top 10, but relevant if the list widens).

**Two outreach postures are in play.** Candidates 1, 3, 4 and 10 are marked *Monitor* — they are principals of competing businesses or too established for the role. **Approaching them as recruitment targets would be a mistake**: it would leak ArtLife's Banksy expansion plans to the best-informed people in the market for no realistic return. For those four the plan below is deliberately a *relationship* strategy, not a recruiting one. Candidates 2, 5, 6, 7 and 9 are genuine approaches.

**A note on discretion.** This is a small market. London's Banksy trade is perhaps thirty people who all know each other. Assume any approach becomes known within weeks, and sequence accordingly: approach your highest-conviction candidate first, not your easiest.

## Cross-cutting: what ArtLife is actually selling

Before the individual plans, the offer itself. Every conversation below rests on four assets that are genuinely distinctive and independently verifiable:

1. **A US collector base that UK Banksy specialists cannot reach.** Almost every strong candidate is UK-based, selling into a saturated domestic market. ArtLife's Miami platform and American collector relationships are the scarcest thing on the table.
2. **A working demand engine, already built.** ArtLife's Banksy editorial — the [investment guide](https://artlife.com/news/banksy-investment-guide/), the [Pest Control authentication guide](https://artlife.com/news/pest-control-authentication/), the [signed vs unsigned analysis](https://artlife.com/news/signed-vs-unsigned-banksy-prints/) — is a functioning inbound machine. A specialist joins a business that already generates enquiries, rather than starting from zero.
3. **A named seat that is currently empty.** ArtLife has no publicly named Banksy specialist. Every competitor does. The candidate would *be* the practice, publicly, from day one — a concrete career asset no employee role at MyArtBroker, Phillips or Heritage can offer.
4. **Founder-to-founder credibility.** Avery Andon came from music and artist management, not the art establishment, and [developed Alec Monopoly's career](https://artlife.com/about/). For candidates who are themselves outsiders — Huw Lougher from KPMG, Danny Herbert from financial markets, Shane Xu from corporate finance, Joseph Bannan from collecting — this is a real point of identification, and it is worth leading with.

**The honest counter-offer to prepare for:** ArtLife is in Miami and the Banksy supply is in Britain. Every serious candidate will raise this. Have a structured answer ready — remote/London-based partner, a UK entity, a travel pattern, or an explicit transatlantic sourcing mandate. Do not improvise it.

# 1. Jasper Tordoff — MyArtBroker *(Priority: Immediate — highest conviction)*

**Best public professional approach channel.** Public LinkedIn profile ([jasper-tordoff-07b600137](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jasper-tordoff-07b600137/)) for a direct, discreet first message — appropriate because he is an employee, not a principal. **Do not route through MyArtBroker's firm contact channels**; that would expose the approach to his employer. A warm introduction via a mutual auction-world contact is materially better if one exists, given his Christie's background.

**Personalised conversation opener.**
> "I watched your Sky News piece on the London Zoo series. You're one of a very small number of people the broadcast media actually calls when Banksy does something — and at ArtLife we've built the Banksy content and the US collector demand, but we've never had a name attached to it. I'd value a confidential conversation about what a Banksy practice looks like when it's yours rather than one desk inside someone else's brand."

**What will appeal to him.** Ownership. He is a specialist inside a firm defined by its founders; at ArtLife he would be the Banksy practice with his name on it. Second, a genuinely new market — MyArtBroker's centre of gravity is UK collectors, and US demand is a book he cannot currently build. Third, cultural continuity: he already works at a technology-led brokerage that positions against traditional galleries, so ArtLife's online-first model reads as familiar.

**Strongest evidence-based reason to approach.** He is the **only publicly recognised Banksy specialist identified who is an employee rather than a business owner.** Syer, Andipa, Cotton and Boroumand all hold equity in competing firms. Tordoff carries an equivalent public credential without the structural barrier to moving — a genuinely rare combination.

**Three questions for the first conversation.**
1. "Walk me through a Banksy transaction you personally originated and closed end-to-end — where did the work come from, and what nearly killed the deal?"
2. "If you were building a Banksy practice from scratch with US demand and no UK supply relationships, what would you do in the first ninety days?"
3. "What would you need to be true — structurally, financially, contractually — for a partnership to be better for you than staying where you are?"

**Diligence and reference plan.** Confirm Christie's employment dates and department directly. Take employment-law advice on restrictive covenants **before** any discussion touching clients. References: a former Christie's Modern British colleague; a collector who has transacted with him (he can introduce, so weight accordingly); an independent auction specialist who has dealt opposite him. Verify the Sky News appearance independently. Probe hard on whether he has ever carried a revenue target — this is the central unknown.

# 2. Danny Herbert — Tate Ward Auctions *(Priority: Immediate)*

**Best public professional approach channel.** Public LinkedIn ([daniel-herbert-a10b0924](https://www.linkedin.com/in/daniel-herbert-a10b0924/)), principal-to-principal. He is a co-owner, so this is a business conversation between two business owners and should be framed that way from the first line. I'd like to talk about what a transatlantic Banksy practice could look like: your supply-side infrastructure, our US collector base. I'm open on structure, including partnership."

**What will appeal to him.** He came to art from twenty years in financial markets, so he will respond to commercial logic, not romance about the trade — lead with economics. He has built things twice and will likely be drawn to a third build with better economics. And Tate Ward's positioning as a disruption of traditional auction models signals real appetite for non-traditional structures.

**Strongest evidence-based reason to approach.** **He is the only candidate in this research with hard, specific published sales figures**: [over £1.3m hammer in the first year of a department he created from nothing](https://www.tateward.com/meet-the-team/), and [eight auctions selling over £13m of art in a single year](https://www.tateward.com/staff/danny-herbert/). Under a brief demanding concrete indicators rather than biographical adjectives, he is the only person who supplies them.

**Three questions for the first conversation.**
1. "The £13m across eight auctions — what was the split between urban and contemporary, what were the sell-through rates, and what did you personally originate?"
2. "You've built consignor relationships auction-side. Which of those would transact privately, and what changes when there's no public sale to anchor the price?"
3. "What does Tate Ward look like in three years, and is there a version where a US partnership makes it stronger rather than competing with it?"

**Diligence and reference plan.** **The sales figures are the diligence.** Verify the £1.3m with a Chiswick Auctions reference who was there, and test whether it was departmental hammer or a personally originated book. Pull Companies House records for Tate Ward — directorships, shareholdings, filed accounts, financial health. References: his Chiswick-era manager; Nigel Shorthouse (his co-founder — handle carefully, since a reference request may itself signal the approach); two repeat consignors. Test explicitly whether auction-side relationships convert to private treaty.

# 3. Taylor Curry — Heritage Auctions, New York *(Priority: Immediate — strongest US option)*

**Best public professional approach channel.** Public LinkedIn ([taylorcurry](https://www.linkedin.com/in/taylorcurry/)). As a US auction director he is reachable through the American fair circuit — Art Basel Miami Beach is the obvious setting and is on ArtLife's home ground.

**Personalised conversation opener.**
> "You were one of the founding specialists on Heritage's Urban Art sales — you helped build that category in the US rather than inheriting it. ArtLife trades the same map you do: Warhol, Haring and Basquiat alongside Banksy, KAWS and RETNA. We have the US demand and the Miami platform; what we don't have is someone who knows how to originate the consignments. I'd like to talk about what building that practice with equity would look like."

**What will appeal to him.** A Sotheby's Institute MA in Art Business suggests someone who thinks about market architecture, not just objects — pitch the business model, not the artworks. After thirteen years inside one institution, the prospect of owning economics rather than executing a department's plan is the live question. And unlike every other strong candidate, **he would not need to relocate across an ocean.**

**Strongest evidence-based reason to approach.** He is the strongest US-based candidate found, and his credentials rest on the most reliable source type collected anywhere in this project — a [formal corporate appointment press release](https://fineart.ha.com/heritage-auctions-press-releases-and-news/taylor-curry-named-director-of-modern-and-contemporary-art-at-heritage-new-york.s?releaseId=4400) plus a documented Junior Specialist → Consignment Director → Director progression. Critically, **his consignment-director background is origination experience**, which addresses ArtLife's actual bottleneck: supply, not demand.

**Three questions for the first conversation.**
1. "As a consignment director, how did you actually originate? Which channels produced the urban art material, and how much was inbound versus hunted?"
2. "Banksy has been in your Urban Art sales — how deep is your personal Banksy knowledge versus the department's, and what would you need to close the gap?"
3. "You've spent thirteen years inside a large institution with its brand, legal team and marketing behind you. What worries you about operating without that?"

**Diligence and reference plan.** **Question three is the whole diligence.** Institutional operators do not always thrive in small entrepreneurial firms, and this is his main risk. Confirm the Heritage appointment and the promotion trail (the press release is checkable). References: a Heritage colleague from his consignment-director period; a consignor he brought in; an Ingleby Gallery contact for the pre-Heritage period. Verify the Sotheby's Institute MA. Test his Banksy depth specifically and honestly — he is an urban art generalist, not a Banksy specialist, and should not be hired as one.

# 4. Rosh Boroumand — GraffitiStreet *(Priority: Immediate)*

**Best public professional approach channel.** [GraffitiStreet's gallery contact and appointment channel](https://graffitistreet.com/) — the gallery publicly invites collectors to book conversations with him directly, which makes it a legitimate professional route. He is a co-owner, so principal-to-principal from the outset.

**Personalised conversation opener.**
> "You spent ten years building GraffitiStreet online before opening the Chichester gallery. ArtLife made the same move — online-only from 2015, Miami flagship in 2019. I'm interested in your approach to provenance and Pest Control certification specifically, because that discipline is the part of the Banksy business most people get wrong, and it's the part we most want to get right at scale in the US."

**What will appeal to him.** Recognition of the craft, not just the sales — his public positioning emphasises provenance research, edition history and condition reporting, which suggests someone who takes pride in rigour. Also scale: a Chichester gallery has a structural ceiling on the collector base it can reach, and ArtLife removes it without asking him to abandon what he built.

**Strongest evidence-based reason to approach.** He combines genuine Banksy specialism with the one competence almost no other candidate can evidence: **disciplined provenance and authentication practice**. For a gallery whose entire proposition rests on verified authenticity, that is the capability that prevents the expensive mistake.

**Three questions for the first conversation.**
1. "Talk me through the most complex provenance problem you've resolved on a Banksy — what was wrong, and how did you establish the chain?"
2. "The six-figure print sourcing you describe — which works, roughly when, and were you principal or broker?"
3. "GraffitiStreet is you and Donna. Is there a structure where ArtLife strengthens it rather than replaces it — affiliation, sourcing partnership, or something more?"

**Diligence and reference plan.** **Question two is essential and slightly uncomfortable — ask it anyway.** The six-figure sourcing claim is promotional copy on his own site with no named works, dates or volumes, and it is the weakest link in an otherwise strong profile. Pull Companies House records for GraffitiStreet — revenue scale and financial health determine whether a move is even conceivable. References: a collector who has bought a significant Banksy through him; a fellow dealer who has traded opposite him; the Chichester gallery's landlord or local press contact for the growth story. **Verify explicitly that he claims no relationship with Pest Control Office** — his material describes navigating the certification process, which is correct and proper, and any drift beyond that in conversation is a red flag.

# 5. Huw Lougher — Lougher Contemporary *(Priority: Immediate)*

**Best public professional approach channel.** [Lougher Contemporary's contact page](https://www.loughercontemporary.com/pages/contact-us), founder to founder. He gives press interviews readily, so he is approachable — but this should be a business conversation, not a media one.

**Personalised conversation opener.**
> "We founded our businesses the same year, on the same bet — that blue-chip secondary market could be sold online properly — and we both came from outside the art world, you from KPMG and me from music management. You've built UK supply relationships over a decade; we've built a US collector base out of Miami. I think there's a conversation about Banksy specifically that's worth both our time."

**What will appeal to him.** The symmetry is genuinely striking and he will see it immediately: same founding year, same model, same outsider origin, complementary geographies. He also speaks publicly with real clear-headedness about the post-2022 downturn, which suggests someone who thinks in cycles and would take seriously an argument about where the market goes next.

**Strongest evidence-based reason to approach.** He is **the closest structural analogue to ArtLife in existence**, and uniquely on this list his credibility comes from independent authority rather than his own marketing — [Artnet News has profiled him twice](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/7-questions-huw-lougher-lougher-contemporary-2331453), [including on market trends](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/lougher-contemporary-art-market-trends-2575434). He has built and sustained an online blue-chip business through a full market cycle.

**Three questions for the first conversation.**
1. "You trade Banksy mostly in the sub-£10k editions band. What would you need to move confidently into originals at six and seven figures?"
2. "What's the hardest thing about selling blue-chip art online that people who haven't done it don't understand?"
3. "Is the interesting structure here a partnership, a transatlantic sourcing arrangement, or something else entirely?"

**Diligence and reference plan.** **Be honest with yourself about what he is.** He is an outstanding operator with thin Banksy credentials — hire him for how he builds and sells, not for Banksy expertise he does not yet have, and do not let the strength of the strategic fit inflate your assessment of his Banksy depth. Pull Companies House records for Lougher Contemporary — revenue, growth, profitability. References: the Artnet editor who profiled him (independent read on his standing); two repeat collectors; a KPMG-era contact on commercial judgement. Test his appetite for the originals market specifically — the step from £10k editions to seven-figure originals is a real one and not everyone wants it.

# 6. Joe Syer — MyArtBroker *(Priority: Monitor — relationship, not recruitment)*

**Recommended posture: do not approach as a recruiter.** He co-owns ArtLife's most direct competitor. A recruiting approach would fail and would signal your Banksy expansion plans to the best-informed operator in the market.

**Best public professional approach channel.** Industry settings only — fairs, panels, podcasts. He appears regularly on broadcast and in market commentary, so a public professional exchange is natural and carries no signalling risk.

**Personalised conversation opener** *(relationship framing)*.
> "Your Banksy market reporting has become genuinely load-bearing infrastructure for how collectors price this artist. We publish on the Banksy market from the US side and see very different collector behaviour to what you describe in the UK. Worth comparing notes some time — the transatlantic divergence is interesting."

**What will appeal to him.** Professional respect for the market reporting, and genuine intellectual interest in US-versus-UK collector behaviour, which is a real phenomenon he cannot observe directly.

**Strongest evidence-based reason to engage.** He is the most complete realisation of the named-specialist model ArtLife should be building. Understanding how MyArtBroker converts market reporting into consignment flow is directly instructive — and observable without hiring anyone.

**Three questions for the first conversation.**
1. "How much of your inbound consignment flow traces back to the market reports versus other channels?"
2. "Where does US collector demand for Banksy differ most from UK demand, in your data?"
3. "Do you ever place works cross-Atlantic, and what makes that hard?"

**Diligence and reference plan.** No recruitment diligence required. Instead, treat him as a **competitive benchmark**: study MyArtBroker's specialist-to-content-to-consignment funnel and assess honestly where ArtLife's editorial is ahead and behind. If a cross-Atlantic referral relationship emerges, that is a real outcome. Keep ArtLife's hiring intentions entirely out of the conversation.

# 7. Acoris Andipa — Andipa Gallery *(Priority: Monitor — institutional relationship)*

**Recommended posture: relationship and learning, not recruitment.** Principal of a family gallery trading since 1967; he is not moving.

**Best public professional approach channel.** [Andipa Gallery's contact page](https://andipagallery.com/), dealer to dealer.

**Personalised conversation opener.**
> "You've used museum exhibitions — Rome, Genoa — as a way of building the collector relationships that eventually produce consignments. We've curated at MOCA Bangkok and ICA Miami but haven't applied that to Banksy. I'd welcome a conversation about how that model actually works, and whether there's anything worth doing together on the US side."

**What will appeal to him.** Recognition of the curatorial dimension of his practice rather than just the dealing, and access to a US institutional and collector network he does not have.

**Strongest evidence-based reason to engage.** He has run the longest continuous Banksy dealing practice identified, and — more usefully — has demonstrated a **supply-side tactic ArtLife could replicate independently**: museum exhibition curation as a consignment-generation engine.

**Three questions for the first conversation.**
1. "How does a museum Banksy exhibition actually get assembled — who lends, and what do they want in return?"
2. "What has changed most in Banksy collector behaviour over your twenty years in it?"
3. "Do you place works into the US market, and what makes that market different to handle?"

**Diligence and reference plan.** No recruitment diligence. **The real output is strategic**: if museum curation reliably generates Banksy consignments, ArtLife should pursue it directly through its existing ICA Miami and MOCA Bangkok relationships — that is potentially the single most actionable insight in this research, and it costs nothing but time.
