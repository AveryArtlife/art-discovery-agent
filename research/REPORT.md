- The **single most reliable signal of a real partnership** in this niche is a
  **branded on-site "creator case"** (WatchGamesTV, ProdigyDDK, JuicyCSGO on

  durable integrations, not one-off reads — and the best outreach anchors.

  Twitch, X and Social Blade all returned anti-bot blocks (HTTP 403) to automated
  fetching. Per the brief we did **not** bypass them, so counts come from search
  snippets where they appeared at all. **Re-verify every number before outreach.**
- **Only one creator has a public business email** (ChrisG → `tiltedtwinz@pm.me`).
  Everyone else routes through a self-published `.gg`/Linktree code hub or has no
  public business contact at all — `reachability` is the binding constraint in
  this niche, not reach.
- **Brand-safety is the headline risk.** Most of these creators frame content as
  gambling ("all in", "world record", loss-chase/redeposit narratives) and
  disclosure is frequently informal or absent. Two of the biggest historical
  names in the niche (TmarTn/CSGOLotto) produced the **FTC's first influencer
  enforcement action**. Vet disclosure compliance per creator and jurisdiction.

## Synthesis: dominant creative patterns

**1. The funnel is identical across every brand.**
> Big-number hook → opening/unboxing → "big-win" reveal → CTA with personal code → leaderboard/giveaway loop.

The personal **referral code doubles as identity and CTA** (the code is usually
the creator's name: `BOUCHON`, `OEG`, `SLOTH`, `JULES`, `CHRISG`, `ORANGIE`).
This makes codes the best discovery tool in the niche — searching a code unspools
the rest of a brand's roster faster than searching the brand.

**2. Hooks are dollar-amount spectacles, not product features.**
Titles lead with stakes and outcomes: "$3,000,000 World Record Unboxing",
"$100,000 Xposed Opening", "ALL IN on Packdraw ($250,000)", "WHEN YOU GO ALL IN
AND IT PAYS MASSIVE". Wins are framed as **record-breaking jackpots or
rarity/value spectacle** (a Lambo, a Rolex, a max-win pull). Losses, when shown,
become **redemption/redeposit narratives** ("I HAD TO REDEPOSIT AND THEN THIS
HAPPENED") — a loss-chase arc that is itself a recurring trope.

**3. The deepest integrations are "creator cases."**
The strongest partners don't just drop a code — the brand builds them a **named
on-site case** (WatchGamesTV/ProdigyDDK/JuicyCSGO on HypeDrop; Xposed on PackDraw;
Aiden/Jules on CSGORoll). Content then becomes "did MY case pay out?", which is
self-reinforcing marketing. **This is the partnership format to emulate** — it
converts a creator into a permanent storefront, not a one-off ad.

**4. Off-platform code hubs are standard.**
Mid/macro creators run their own `.gg` site or Linktree (`oeg.gg`,
`coderydurz.com`, `slothrewards.com`, `aidengambles.gg`, `bouchonnoir.gg`,
`linktr.ee/watchgamestv`) hosting the code + a wager leaderboard with cash/prize
incentives. These hubs are the **only reliable public contact surface** in a
niche where almost no one lists a business email.

**5. Disclosure is inconsistent — and a real liability.**
Disclosure ranged from explicit ("Excited to announce my new sponsorship with
@PackDraw") to informal ("working for my friend doom13") to absent. We could not
read most video descriptions (blocked), so disclosure compliance is **unverified
for the majority** and must be checked per creator. The cautionary baseline:
TmarTn/ProSyndicate's undisclosed CSGOLotto promotion drew the **FTC's first-ever
influencer enforcement action (2017)**.

### Which formats correlate with the biggest creators?

| Tier | Dominant format | Primary platform | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Macro | **Dedicated long-form video + branded creator case** | YouTube | The biggest names anchor on owned, evergreen video assets and on-site cases. |
| Mid | Long-form video **+ Shorts**, plus live | YouTube/Twitch (+TikTok) | Hybrid: long-form for depth, Shorts/clips for reach. |
| Micro | **Livestream-led** (slots/openings) | **Kick** + Twitch | Kick is the default live venue for gambling content; daily streams + leaderboard grind. |
| Nano–micro | **Short-form clip volume** | TikTok | Code-as-hook battle/win clips; cheap, high-frequency, low individual reach. |

**Read:** reach scales with **owned long-form assets and on-site integration**;
the long tail is **short-form TikTok** and **live Kick** code-drops. If the goal
is durable brand equity, buy creator-case integrations from the long-form tier;
if the goal is cheap volume and code distribution, buy the TikTok/Kick tail.

- **Doom13** (`@DoomisGod` / `@DoomPackdraw`, YT `@Doom13`) — he is the **owner/

- **ohnePixel** (Twitch/YT/X, macro CS2 skin streamer) — a skin/case **enthusiast
  and commentator who critiques** the gambling ecosystem (reacted to Coffeezilla's
  exposé). Not a current promoter; no verified active gambling code. Brand-safety
  signal, not a target.
- **Anomaly** (Ludwig Lagerstedt, macro) — historically did big CS:GO case openings
  but is widely cited as a **critic/whistleblower** of skin-gambling sites. Do not
  attribute an active code to him.
- **TmarTn (Trevor Martin) + ProSyndicate (Tom Cassell)** — the **CSGOLotto** case:
  secretly owned the site they promoted without disclosure → **FTC's first
  influencer enforcement action (2017)**. The cautionary disclosure baseline for
  any campaign in this category. Legacy, not active.

1. **Re-verify live, in a normal browser session:** follower counts, current
   activity, exact code strings, and—critically—**disclosure language** for the
   top ~10 (every count here is snippet-sourced or `ESTIMATE`).

4. **Stand up a brand-safety/legal gate before any spend** — gambling-style
   framing, jurisdictional ad rules, and the FTC disclosure precedent make
   compliance review non-optional in this category.
5. **Use codes as your expansion engine** — each confirmed creator code, searched
   directly, unspools more of that brand's affiliate roster for round two.
