## Executive summary (what changed in 2025–2026 and why it matters)

1. **The FDA peptide landscape flipped twice in 2026.** After the Sept 2023 Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) process placed ~19 popular "longevity" peptides (BPC-157, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, GHK-Cu, AOD-9604, MOTS-c, thymosin alpha-1, TB-500, selank, semax, KPV, etc.) into **503A Category 2** ("significant safety risks" — effectively un-compoundable), HHS Secretary Kennedy announced on **Feb 27, 2026** that roughly **14 of the 19** would be moved out of Category 2; FDA updated its 503A bulks document on **Apr 15, 2026** (effective ~Apr 23, 2026), and the **PCAC met Jul 23–24, 2026**, narrowly recommending **BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, semax and epitalon** for the 503A bulks list *over the objections of FDA career scientists*. **Melanotan II, GHRP-2, GHRP-6, LL-37 and PEG-MGF remain Category 2.** Nothing here is FDA *approval*; final bulks-list rulemaking is still pending, and the political durability of the reversal is uncertain. Product/claims strategy must be built to survive either direction. (Sources: Pharmacy Times, AJMC, STAT, NPR, PharmExec, Newtropin — third-party reports; primary FDA page not reachable this session.)
2. **"Research use only" (RUO) is a closed loophole.** FDA issued seven warning letters dated **Mar 31, 2026** (published Apr 7, 2026) to online sellers of GLP-1 copycats, BPC-157 and SARMs labeled RUO, holding that RUO disclaimers do not negate *intended use* when the site describes human effects. Lilly has separately sued RUO sellers of retatrutide. **Model A must never sell or affiliate-link RUO peptides.**
3. **Compounded GLP-1s are in wind-down.** Semaglutide's shortage was declared resolved Feb 2025 (tirzepatide Dec 2024); FDA issued **55+ warning letters (Sept 16, 2025), ~30 to telehealth companies (Mar 2026) and ~25 more (Jun 2026)** for misleading marketing; on **Apr 30, 2026** FDA proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide from the 503B bulks list; **>1,700 adverse events** logged as of May 21, 2026. Novo has filed **130+ suits in 40 states**; Novo sued **Hims & Hers (Feb 2026)** and dismissed after a Wegovy partnership (Mar 2026). Brand-name DTC channels (**LillyDirect $299–$449; NovoCare $199 intro → $349; TrumpRx ~$350 avg; Wegovy pill $149**) have collapsed the price gap that justified compounding. **Model B should route GLP-1 demand to branded DTC/affiliate channels, not compounded product.**
4. **FTC is aggressive on telehealth marketing and subscriptions even without Click-to-Cancel.** The 8th Circuit vacated the rule on **Jul 8, 2025**; FTC restarted rulemaking with an **ANPRM on Mar 11, 2026 (comments closed Apr 13, 2026)**, and continues to enforce under **ROSCA**. **NextMed** (final order **Dec 3, 2025**, $150,000) is the template case: hidden fees, fake reviews, fabricated before/after, unsubstantiated weight-loss claims. FTC and a group of states have also sued **Hims & Hers** over health-data sharing and subscription billing (2026, per Shumaker alert — details unverified). **Model C's auto-renewal flow and Model A/B's testimonials are the two highest-probability FTC exposures.**
5. **Ad platforms and payments are the practical bottleneck.** Meta's health-and-wellness "data source" restrictions (from Jan/Feb 2025, expanding into 2026) block purchase-event optimization for health-classified accounts; Google requires **LegitScript/NABP certification** for prescription-drug/telehealth ads and (as of **Mar 31, 2026**) allows recurring-billing Rx promotions only for certified merchants; **Stripe** prohibits "pharmaceuticals and pseudo-pharmaceuticals," "nutraceuticals" and unapproved research chemicals and treats telemedicine as restricted; **Mastercard's 2026 BRAM update** tightened peptide/nutraceutical enforcement. **A high-risk-capable acquirer and LegitScript certification are pre-launch dependencies for Model B.**
6. **Privacy exposure is federal + state.** FTC Health Breach Notification Rule (amended 2024) reaches non-HIPAA health apps; Washington MHMDA (private right of action), Nevada and Connecticut consumer-health-data laws apply to Model A quizzes and Model C memberships; New York's NYHIPA was **vetoed Dec 19, 2025** but reintroduced (**S9269/A10357**) in 2026 and remains pending. TCPA one-to-one consent was vacated (11th Cir., Jan 24, 2025) and repealed by the FCC, but core TCPA consent/revocation rules and state mini-TCPAs remain.
7. **Structural risk for Model B is state law, not federal.** CPOM/fee-splitting (CA, NY, TX, NJ), telehealth consent and modality rules (states restricting questionnaire-only prescribing), non-resident pharmacy licensure for fulfillment partners, and state peptide/GLP-1 board rules (e.g., Louisiana Board of Pharmacy restrictions; Louisiana SB 253 (2026) moving the other way) require a state-by-state launch map.

## 1. FDA status of peptides and GLP-1s

### 1.1 The 503A bulks-list framework (why it matters)
Under FDCA §503A, a state-licensed pharmacy or physician may compound with a bulk drug substance only if it (i) has a USP/NF monograph, (ii) is a component of an FDA-approved drug, or (iii) appears on FDA's 503A bulks list (established by rulemaking after PCAC review). Pending rulemaking, FDA sorts nominated substances into **Category 1** (may be compounded under interim enforcement discretion), **Category 2** ("may present significant safety risks" — no enforcement discretion, i.e., compounding is treated as unlawful), and **Category 3** (insufficient nomination support). *(Analyst inference — framework; FDA primary page not reachable this session.)*

**Business-model impact:** determines whether Model B can lawfully have *any* non-GLP-1 peptide prescribed and compounded; determines what Model A may even *describe* as available.

### 1.2 Sept 2023 PCAC / Category 2 placements
In September–October 2023 FDA placed a group of nominated peptides in Category 2 following PCAC review, including (per the widely-reported list of ~19): **AOD-9604, BPC-157, CJC-1295, dihexa, DSIP, epitalon, GHK-Cu (injectable), GHRP-2, GHRP-6, ipamorelin, kisspeptin-10, KPV, LL-37, melanotan II, MOTS-c, PEG-MGF, selank, semax, thymosin alpha-1 (Ta1), thymosin beta-4/TB-500.** *(Third-party report + analyst inference on exact composition — see Section 11 for verification.)*
- **PT-141 (bremelanotide)** is *not* a bulks-list question: it is the API of FDA-approved **Vyleesi** (2019), so compounding from it is governed by the "component of an approved drug" pathway and the §503A "essentially a copy" limits. *(Analyst inference.)*
- **Sermorelin**: the approved product (Geref) was discontinued; because it was once a component of an approved drug, it has been treated as compoundable under §503A, and it remained widely compounded through 2024–2026. *(Analyst inference — confirm current FDA position.)*
- **Tesamorelin (Egrifta / Egrifta WR)** is FDA-approved (HIV lipodystrophy) and is Rx-only via its manufacturer; off-label "longevity" use is a prescriber decision, but marketing it for anti-aging is an off-label promotion risk for the brand. *(Analyst inference.)*

Legal challenges and a possible policy reversal under future leadership are live risks (safehg.com reports litigation activity).

### 1.4 RUO peptides — enforcement posture (2023–2026)
- FDA has issued successive waves of warning letters to online "research peptide" sellers (2023–2025), culminating in **seven letters dated Mar 31, 2026 (published Apr 7, 2026)** targeting sellers of RUO semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide, BPC-157 and SARMs (e.g., trendione). FDA's stated position: "research use only" and "not for human consumption" disclaimers are irrelevant when product pages describe appetite suppression, weight loss, healing, etc. — the products are unapproved new drugs and misbranded based on intended use. *(Third-party reports: Health Law Alliance, PeptideJournal, PolicyCanary, PeptIQ.)*
- **Lilly** has filed suits against RUO sellers of **retatrutide** (an investigational, unapproved Lilly molecule), using Lanham Act/unfair-competition theories. *(Third-party report: Frier Levitt.)*
- Payment networks mirror this: Stripe/PayPal/Square shut down RUO peptide merchants; Mastercard's 2026 BRAM update tightened enforcement. *(Third-party report.)*

**Implication:** an "education/commerce" model that sells, drop-ships, or affiliate-links RUO peptides is **High** risk under FDCA (unapproved new drug/misbranding), FTC Act (deceptive), state UDAP, and platform/payment policies — regardless of disclaimers.

### 1.5 Compounded GLP-1s after shortage resolution
- **Shortage resolution:** tirzepatide (Dec 2024); semaglutide (Feb 2025). FDA's enforcement-discretion wind-down periods for 503A/503B compounders expired in spring 2025. *(Verified fact via multiple consistent reports; primary FDA notice not opened.)*
- **Enforcement:** Sept 16, 2025 — 55+ warning letters to online sellers of compounded GLP-1s; Mar 2026 — ~30 to telehealth companies; Jun 2026 — ~25 more. Violations cited were **marketing** violations — false/misleading claims (e.g., implying FDA approval, "same as Ozempic," safety/efficacy, personalization claims, RUO). *(Third-party reports: Venable, Sheppard Mullin, Pharmacy Times, iMedic.)*
- **Apr 30, 2026:** FDA proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide from the **503B bulks list**, citing no clinical need — closing the bulk-API pathway for outsourcing facilities. *(Third-party report: Pharmacy Times, OnHealthcare.tech.)*
- **Adverse events:** >1,700 reported for compounded semaglutide/tirzepatide as of May 21, 2026. *(Third-party report.)*
- **Remaining lawful compounding** is limited to patient-specific clinical need (e.g., documented allergy to an excipient, dose not commercially available) under §503A, with "essentially a copy" analysis — a narrow lane with heavy documentation burden and now a demonstrated warning-letter risk. *(Analyst inference.)*

### 1.6 Approved peptide/GLP-1 products and DTC pricing (context for positioning)
- **Approved:** semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus; oral Wegovy reported on the market at $149/mo in 2026), tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound), liraglutide (Victoza/Saxenda + generics), tesamorelin (Egrifta), bremelanotide (Vyleesi), teriparatide (Forteo + follow-ons). *(Analyst inference except pricing.)*
- **DTC cash channels (2026):** **TrumpRx** launched Jan 2026 with GLP-1 vials at ~$350/month average (manufacturer MFN deal, Nov 2025); **LillyDirect** Zepbound lowest dose $299 → higher doses up to $449 (down from $349/$499); **NovoCare** Wegovy $199 intro for two fills (through Jun 30, 2026) then $349; Wegovy pill $149 (4 mg promo through Aug 31, 2026, then $199); Medicare injectable price $245. *(Third-party reports: AMCP, NBC News, Mercer, Affinity Whole Health.)*
- **Implication:** a premium brand can lawfully participate in GLP-1 demand via *affiliate/education* pointing to branded DTC (Model A), or via *prescribing branded product* (Model B) — not via compounded copies.

## 2. FTC advertising, subscriptions and health privacy

### 2.1 Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised June 2023) and Consumer Reviews Rule (16 CFR Part 465, effective Oct 21, 2024)
- Material connections include **ownership/equity, employment, payment, free product, family relationships**; a celebrity co-founder/investor must disclose ownership when endorsing. Disclosures must be clear, conspicuous, unavoidable, and in the same medium (in-video/audio, not only in bio or a hashtag string). Advertisers are liable for endorsers' claims and must monitor. "Results not typical" no longer cures an atypical testimonial — generally expected results must be disclosed. *(Analyst inference — primary FTC page not reachable; consistent with 2023 revision.)*
- The 2024 **Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule** makes fake/AI-generated reviews, review suppression, and undisclosed insider reviews civil-penalty violations (~$53,088 per violation, inflation-adjusted). *(Analyst inference.)*
- **NextMed (2025)** shows the FTC applying these to telehealth: fake reviews, before/after photos of non-customers, and fake testimonials. *(Verified fact — FTC press releases surfaced in search: Jul 14, 2025 proposed; Dec 3, 2025 final; $150,000.)*

### 2.2 Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022)
Health claims need **competent and reliable scientific evidence**, typically randomized, controlled human clinical testing for efficacy claims; the guidance covers substantiation of implied claims, disease claims, consumer testimonials (treated as efficacy claims), and disclosures. It is the yardstick for every peptide "benefit" statement (healing, recovery, longevity, fat loss, cognition). *(Analyst inference — page not reachable.)* For unapproved peptides there is essentially **no** RCT evidence in humans (sources uniformly note "limited data"), so **efficacy claims cannot be substantiated** — Model A content must be framed as education about emerging research with explicit no-approval statements, not as product benefit claims.

### 2.3 Negative option / Click-to-Cancel / ROSCA / state auto-renewal laws
- **Federal rule status:** 8th Circuit **vacated** the Click-to-Cancel rule **Jul 8, 2025** (procedural — missing preliminary regulatory analysis). FTC sent a draft ANPRM to OIRA **Jan 30, 2026** and published an **ANPRM Mar 11, 2026** (comments due **Apr 13, 2026**) asking whether to amend the 1973 Negative Option Rule and revive click-to-cancel elements. No new rule is in force as of this brief. *(Third-party reports: Crowell, Gibson Dunn, Kelley Drye, Latham.)*
- **ROSCA (15 U.S.C. §8403)** remains fully enforceable: clear disclosure of material terms before billing, express informed consent, simple cancellation. **Hims & Hers** (FTC + states, 2026) and **NextMed** both invoke subscription practices. *(Third-party report / Verified fact respectively.)*
- **California:** AB 2863 amendments to the Automatic Renewal Law (Bus. & Prof. Code §17600 et seq.) took effect **Jul 1, 2025** — affirmative consent to auto-renewal terms, cancellation in the same medium as sign-up, annual reminders for programs ≥ 1 year, and click-to-cancel mechanics for online enrollments. Other states (NY GBL §527-a, and 2024–2026 updates in e.g. Minnesota, Utah, Virginia, Tennessee) impose similar duties. *(Analyst inference — not re-verified this session.)*

### 2.4 Health Breach Notification Rule (16 CFR Part 318, amended 2024)
FTC finalized amendments in **April 2024** (effective **July 29, 2024**) clarifying that non-HIPAA **health apps and similar technologies** are "vendors of personal health records," that "breach of security" includes **unauthorized disclosures** (e.g., ad-pixel sharing), and expanding notice content/timing (within 60 days; FTC notice for ≥500 individuals). Precedents: **GoodRx (Feb 2023, $1.5M — first HBNR action), BetterHelp (Mar 2023, $7.8M), Cerebral (Apr 2024, ~$7M)** — all involving sharing health data with Meta/Google/TikTok pixels. *(Analyst inference — page not reachable; dates from background knowledge.)*
**Implication:** a longevity quiz, symptom intake, or membership health profile on the marketing site is a PHR under the HBNR even if HIPAA doesn't apply (Model A/C); Model B's clinical side is HIPAA (covered entity/business associate) and both regimes may apply to different data flows.

## 3. State law: practice structure, telehealth, pharmacy, privacy, marketing channels

### 3.1 Corporate practice of medicine (CPOM) & MSO / "friendly PC"
- **California:** strong CPOM (B&P §§2052, 2400; Medical Board guidance). Lay MSO may not control clinical decisions, hire/fire clinicians for clinical reasons, set clinical protocols, or own the PC; **2025 legislation (AB 1415)** brought MSOs under OHCA transaction notice regime. *(Analyst inference.)*
- **New York:** strict CPOM (Educ. Law §6530; BCL Art. 15); only NY-licensed physicians may own a PC; percentage-of-revenue MSO fees are treated as fee-splitting (§6530(19)); NY also attaches CPOM consequences to insurance/no-fault contexts (Mallela). *(Analyst inference.)*
- **Texas:** strong CPOM (Occ. Code §§164.052(a)(13), 165.156); friendly-PC via 501a nonprofit health organization or MSO with strict clinical-independence carve-outs. *(Analyst inference.)*
- **New Jersey:** N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.16 governs professional practice structure and prohibits fee-splitting; MSO permitted on fixed/FMV fees; Board of Medical Examiners rules on advertising and telehealth also apply. *(Analyst inference.)*
- **Trend:** 2025–2026 state bills (e.g., **Oregon SB 951**, enacted 2025) tighten MSO control features (stock-transfer restriction agreements, MSO ownership by clinicians) — a signal that "friendly PC" structures are under scrutiny nationally. *(Analyst inference.)*

### 3.2 Fee-splitting
State fee-splitting statutes (CA B&P §650; NY Educ. Law §6531; TX Occ. Code ch. 102; NJ 13:35-6.16(f)) restrict percentage-based compensation between the MSO/brand and the prescriber, and marketing fees tied to prescriptions. Federal Anti-Kickback applies only where federal payors are involved (mostly not in cash-pay), but the **Eliminating Kickbacks in Recovery Act** and state all-payor statutes (e.g., FL, TX, MA) can reach cash-pay referrals. *(Analyst inference.)*

### 3.3 Telehealth consent and modality
- Most states require **informed consent to telehealth** (e.g., CA B&P §2290.5 — verbal or written; TX Occ. Code §111.002) and prescriber licensure in the **patient's** state. *(Analyst inference.)*
- **Asynchronous/questionnaire-only prescribing** is permitted in many states but restricted in others (some require a synchronous audio-video or in-person encounter before prescribing, or bar questionnaire-only for a first prescription; boards in several states have disciplined questionnaire-only GLP-1 prescribing). Build a state matrix: sync-required / async-allowed / async-allowed-with-conditions. *(Analyst inference — could not fetch CCHP tracker.)*
- **Ryan Haight Act / DEA:** the DEA/HHS **Fourth Temporary Extension** (Federal Register, Dec 31, 2025, doc. 2025-24123) keeps COVID-era tele-prescribing flexibilities for Schedule II–V controlled substances through **Dec 31, 2026**; the Jan 2025 special-registration proposal is not finalized. **Peptides on the bulks list, GLP-1s, and most longevity agents are not controlled substances**, so DEA telemedicine rules do not apply — *unless* the formulary adds testosterone/anabolic steroids (Schedule III) or other controlled agents (HGH is separately restricted under 21 U.S.C. §333(e)). *(Verified fact for the extension; analyst inference for scheduling.)*

### 3.4 Pharmacy licensure and fulfillment
- Non-resident pharmacy licensure is required in essentially every state into which a pharmacy ships; 503A compounders shipping interstate face FDA's §503A(b)(3)(B) interstate-distribution limits (5% of total Rx orders absent a state MOU; 50% under the FDA–state MOU). Verify NABP/state licenses, sterile compounding (USP <797>) status, and any state board restrictions (e.g., **Louisiana Board of Pharmacy** restrictions on semaglutide-class compounding). *(Analyst inference + third-party report for Louisiana.)*
- **Louisiana SB 253 (2026)** would bar state boards from prohibiting prescribers/pharmacists from providing peptides sourced from compliant 503A/503B facilities — status unverified. **Mississippi AG** issued consumer warnings on compounded GLP-1s (sublingual/nasal forms). **38 AGs/jurisdictions** (led by SC, CO, IL, TN) urged FDA in 2025 to increase enforcement against illegal compounded GLP-1s and partner with state boards. *(Third-party reports: Sheppard Mullin, PeptideLaws, NAAG, Stevens & Lee.)*

## 5. Payments

- **Stripe** (Restricted Businesses): prohibits "pharmaceuticals and pseudo-pharmaceuticals," "nutraceuticals" making unsafe/harmful claims, and unapproved research chemicals; online pharmacies and **telemedicine** are restricted (case-by-case approval); RUO peptides are treated as prohibited and accounts are closed with 90–180-day reserves. *(Third-party reports: DirectPayNet, PayKings, Bloom, Akord, InclusivePay.)*
- **Shopify Payments** (Stripe-powered): its prohibited/restricted list similarly excludes pharmaceuticals/pseudo-pharmaceuticals and nutraceuticals with unsubstantiated claims; peptides are routinely flagged. *(Analyst inference — AUP page not reachable.)*
- **Card networks:** Mastercard's 2026 BRAM update (reported as GLB 11691.1) tightened controls over research peptides, unapproved pharmaceuticals and nutraceuticals; Visa Integrity Risk Program applies similar high-risk registration. Expect **MCC 5122/5912/8099**, high-risk registration fees, rolling reserves (5–15%), and chargeback thresholds (<0.9–1%). *(Third-party report + analyst inference.)*
- **Implication:** Model A supplement commerce can likely stay on mainstream processors *if* claims are compliant; Model B and any peptide SKU need a high-risk-experienced acquirer (e.g., processors specializing in telehealth) with LegitScript in hand; Model C's recurring billing must meet network negative-option rules (Visa/Mastercard trial-and-subscription mandates) in addition to ROSCA.

## 6. Right of publicity, NIL and celebrity endorsement contracts

- **FTC:** an owner/investor celebrity is materially connected; every endorsement (including "founder story" content) needs a clear disclosure of the ownership interest; the brand is liable for the celebrity's unsubstantiated statements. *(Analyst inference on Guides text; consistent with 2023 revision.)*
- **California:** Civ. Code **§3344** (statutory; $750 minimum or actual damages, plus profits and attorney fees) and common-law right of publicity; **§3344.1** post-mortem; 2024 laws (AB 2602, AB 1836) regulate **digital replicas** of performers. *(Analyst inference.)*
- **New York:** Civil Rights Law **§§50–51** (written consent required for advertising/trade use of name, portrait, picture, voice); **§50-f** post-mortem right (2021); 2024–2025 digital-replica amendments. *(Analyst inference.)*
- **Contract norms:** exclusivity by category (peptides/longevity/telehealth), approval rights over claims, mandatory FTC-disclosure clause and compliance training, indemnities, **morality clause** (conduct bringing brand into disrepute; increasingly **reverse morality clauses** protecting talent from brand misconduct — highly relevant given regulatory volatility), term/termination on regulatory change, and NIL usage grant with territory/media/duration and AI-replica carve-outs. *(Analyst inference from standard industry practice.)*

## 7. Liability and insurance

- **Professional liability:** telehealth prescribers need malpractice coverage that expressly covers telemedicine, all states of licensure, and (critically) **off-label/unapproved compounded peptides and GLP-1s** — some carriers exclude or sub-limit these. Typical limits $1M/$3M per clinician; the PC needs an entity policy. *(Analyst inference.)*
- **Product liability:** compounding pharmacies carry product exposure, but brands/MSOs marketing the product face negligent-marketing/apparent-manufacturer claims and are named alongside pharmacies (the GLP-1 MDL against Novo/Lilly — >3,200 federal cases in E.D. Pa. as of Apr 2026 — shows the plaintiff-bar appetite). Require partner pharmacies to carry ≥$5M products/completed-ops and name the brand as additional insured. *(Third-party report for MDL; analyst inference for coverage.)*
- **D&O, E&O, Cyber:** D&O for regulatory investigations (FDA/FTC/state AG defense costs); tech E&O/media liability for advertising injury and right-of-publicity claims; **cyber** with regulatory-fines coverage (HBNR/HIPAA/state), typical $2–5M for a DTC health brand; check exclusions for "unlawful collection" (pixel litigation) and wrongful-collection sub-limits. *(Analyst inference.)*
- **Adverse-event handling:** FDA MedWatch reporting for compounded products flows through the pharmacy, but the brand needs an AE intake, triage and escalation SOP with medical-director sign-off. *(Analyst inference.)*

## 8. Accessibility

- **Litigation:** 3,117 federal ADA Title III web-accessibility suits in 2025 (+27% vs 2024; second-highest ever), 36% of all federal Title III filings (8,667); >5,000 including state courts (NY and CA state courts are heavy venues). *(Third-party report: Seyfarth ADA Title III blog, Level Access, Accessibility.build.)*
- **Standard:** WCAG 2.1 AA remains the litigation benchmark; WCAG 2.2 AA is best practice. **DOJ Title II rule (Apr 2024)** adopted WCAG 2.1 AA for state/local governments (context only; an Apr 20, 2026 interim final rule extended compliance deadlines by one year). *(Third-party report.)*
- **Implication (all models):** premium DTC health sites are frequent targets; conform to WCAG 2.2 AA at build time, run automated + manual audits, publish an accessibility statement, and make video/telehealth flows keyboard/screen-reader operable.

## 10. Recommended launch sequencing (analyst view)

1. **Phase 0 (all models):** claims library + substantiation files; consumer-health-data privacy program (WA/NV/CT/CA); WCAG 2.2 AA build; ROSCA/CA-ARL checkout; influencer/celebrity contracts with disclosure SOP; processor selection with health-vertical appetite.
2. **Phase 1 (Model A):** education + approved-drug affiliate (LillyDirect/NovoCare/TrumpRx) + compliant supplements (tested, no disease claims). **No peptides for sale.**
3. **Phase 2 (Model B):** MSO/PC in 2–3 CPOM-friendly states first; synchronous initial visits; LegitScript; 503A partners with licensure warranties; formulary limited to (i) approved drugs and (ii) peptides that reach the final 503A bulks list or hold clear Category 1 status, with change control; **no compounded GLP-1s.**
4. **Phase 3 (Model C):** membership defined as services; benefits never framed as "access to [peptide]"; annual regulatory review.
